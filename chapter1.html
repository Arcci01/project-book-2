Chapter 1: Introduction to Computer and Computer System 1.1 Introduction of Computer A computer is an electronic device that accepts data as input, processes it according to a set of instructions, stores data and information, and produces meaningful results as output. Computers are widely used in education, business, communication, banking, healthcare, science, entertainment, and many other fields. 1.1.1 Definition, Characteristics and Applications of Computer Definition: A computer is a programmable electronic machine that receives data, processes it, stores it, and produces information as output. Characteristics of a Computer Speed: A computer can perform millions or billions of operations in a second. Accuracy: It produces highly accurate results when the instructions and data are correct. Storage: It can store large amounts of data and retrieve it when required. Diligence: A computer can perform repetitive tasks without becoming tired. Versatility: It can perform many different types of tasks. Automation: Once programmed, it can perform tasks automatically. Reliability: It can perform operations consistently and reliably. Large Capacity: Modern computers can handle huge volumes of data. Applications of Computers Computers are used in: Education: Online learning, examinations, research and presentations. Business: Accounting, payroll, inventory and data management. Banking: Online banking, ATMs and electronic transactions. Healthcare: Patient records, diagnosis and medical research. Communication: Email, video conferencing and social media. Science and Engineering: Simulation, design and research. Entertainment: Games, movies, music and digital content. Government: Record keeping, public services and administration. 1.1.2 Evolution of Computer Technology The development of computers can be divided into several generations. First Generation (1940–1956) These computers used vacuum tubes. They were very large, expensive, consumed much electricity and produced considerable heat. Examples include ENIAC and UNIVAC. Second Generation (1956–1963) Transistors replaced vacuum tubes. Computers became smaller, faster, more reliable and more energy-efficient. Third Generation (1964–1971) Integrated Circuits (ICs) were introduced. Many electronic components could be placed on a single chip, making computers smaller and more powerful. Fourth Generation (1971–Present) The invention of the microprocessor led to personal computers, laptops and other compact computing devices. Computers became affordable and widely available. Fifth Generation The fifth generation focuses on advanced technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning, natural language processing, robotics and parallel processing. 1.1.3 Measurement Unit of Processing Speed and Storage Unit Processing Speed The speed of a processor is commonly measured in Hertz (Hz), which represents cycles per second. 1 Hz = 1 cycle per second 1 KHz = 1,000 Hz 1 MHz = 1,000,000 Hz 1 GHz = 1,000,000,000 Hz For example, a processor with a clock speed of 3 GHz performs approximately three billion clock cycles per second. Storage Units Computer storage is measured in bytes and larger units: Unit Approximate Value Bit 0 or 1 1 Byte (B) 8 bits 1 Kilobyte (KB) 1,024 bytes 1 Megabyte (MB) 1,024 KB 1 Gigabyte (GB) 1,024 MB 1 Terabyte (TB) 1,024 GB 1 Petabyte (PB) 1,024 TB A bit is the smallest unit of digital data and can have a value of 0 or 1. 1.1.4 Super, Mainframe, Mini and Microcomputers Computers can be classified according to their processing power, size and purpose. Supercomputer A supercomputer is an extremely powerful computer designed to perform complex calculations at very high speed. It is used for weather forecasting, scientific research, space research and simulations. Mainframe Computer A mainframe is a powerful computer designed to process large volumes of data and support many users simultaneously. Banks, airlines and large organizations commonly use mainframes. Minicomputer A minicomputer is a mid-range computer that is more powerful than traditional microcomputers but smaller than mainframes. Historically, such systems were used by organizations and laboratories. Microcomputer A microcomputer is a computer based on a microprocessor and generally designed for individual use. Desktop computers, laptops, tablets and many personal computing devices fall into this broad category. 1.1.5 Mobile Computing and Its Applications Mobile computing refers to the use of computing technology while moving or without being connected to a fixed location. It commonly involves smartphones, tablets, laptops, wireless networks and cloud services. Applications of Mobile Computing Mobile banking and digital payments Online education GPS and navigation Video conferencing Social networking Mobile healthcare Online shopping Remote work Entertainment and gaming Field data collection Mobile computing provides portability, flexibility, wireless connectivity and access to information from almost anywhere. 1.2 Computer System and I/O Devices A computer system consists of hardware, software, data, users and procedures that work together to perform computing tasks. Input and output devices allow users to communicate with the computer. 1.2.1 Concept of Computer Architecture and Organization Computer architecture refers to the functional design and conceptual structure of a computer system, including how its major components work from the programmer's perspective. Computer organization refers to the way the physical components are arranged and implemented to realize the architecture. In simple terms: Architecture describes what the computer does. Organization describes how the computer implements it. 1.2.2 Components of Computer System A computer system mainly consists of the following components: 1. Input Unit The input unit accepts data and instructions from the user and sends them to the computer for processing. Examples: Keyboard, mouse, scanner and microphone. 2. Processing Unit The Central Processing Unit (CPU) processes data and controls other components. It consists mainly of the Control Unit (CU), Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU), and registers. 3. Memory Unit Memory stores data, instructions and intermediate results needed during processing. 4. Storage Storage provides long-term data retention. Examples include hard disks, SSDs, USB flash drives and optical disks. 5. Output Unit The output unit presents processed information to the user. Examples: Monitor, printer and speaker. 1.2.3 Microprocessor A microprocessor is a programmable electronic chip that contains the main processing functions of a CPU on a single integrated circuit. Clock Speed Clock speed indicates the number of clock cycles a processor can perform per second. It is commonly expressed in MHz or GHz. Word Length Word length is the number of bits a processor can process or handle as a unit. Common examples include 8-bit, 16-bit, 32-bit and 64-bit processors. Main Components ALU: Performs arithmetic and logical operations. Control Unit: Directs and coordinates operations. Registers: Small, high-speed storage locations inside the processor. Cache: High-speed memory used to store frequently needed data and instructions. Functions The microprocessor: Fetches instructions. Decodes instructions. Executes instructions. Performs calculations and logical operations. Controls the flow of data between different components. 1.2.4 Bus System A bus is a communication pathway used to transfer data, addresses and control signals between components of a computer. Data Bus Carries actual data between the processor, memory and other devices. It is generally bidirectional. Address Bus Carries the address of the memory location or I/O location being accessed. It is generally unidirectional from the processor. Control Bus Carries control and timing signals that coordinate operations, such as read, write and interrupt signals. 1.2.5 Primary Memory Primary memory is the memory directly accessible by the CPU. It stores data and instructions needed for current processing. RAM (Random Access Memory) RAM is a volatile memory, meaning its contents are normally lost when power is turned off. Types of RAM include: SRAM (Static RAM): Fast and does not require periodic refreshing; commonly used for cache. DRAM (Dynamic RAM): Requires periodic refreshing and is commonly used as main memory. ROM (Read Only Memory) ROM is generally non-volatile, meaning it retains data when power is removed. It stores permanent or semi-permanent instructions. Types include: PROM: Programmable once. EPROM: Can be erased using ultraviolet light and reprogrammed. EEPROM: Can be electrically erased and reprogrammed. Cache Memory Cache is a small, very high-speed memory located in or close to the CPU. It stores frequently used data and instructions to reduce access time. Buffer A buffer is a temporary memory area used to hold data while it is being transferred between devices or processes operating at different speeds. 1.2.6 Secondary Memory Secondary memory is non-volatile storage used to store programs and data for long-term use. It generally has greater capacity than primary memory but is slower to access. Magnetic Disk Magnetic disks store data using magnetic patterns. Examples: Hard Disk Drives (HDDs) and older floppy disks. Flash Memory Flash memory is non-volatile electronic storage with no moving mechanical parts. Examples: SSDs, USB flash drives and memory cards. Optical Disk Optical disks use laser technology to read and write data. Examples: CD, DVD and Blu-ray Disc. External Storage Devices External storage devices provide additional or portable storage. Examples: External HDDs, external SSDs, USB flash drives and memory cards. Memory Stick A memory stick generally refers to a small portable flash-storage device. The term may also specifically refer to Sony's Memory Stick family of removable storage media. 1.2.7 Input Devices Input devices are used to enter data and instructions into a computer. Keyboard A keyboard is used to enter text, numbers, symbols and commands. Mouse A mouse is a pointing device used to select, open, move and interact with objects on the screen. Scanner A scanner converts printed documents or images into digital form. Light Pen A light pen is a pointing device that can detect a specific position on a display. It was used mainly with older display systems. OMR (Optical Mark Recognition) OMR detects marked areas on specially designed forms, such as answer sheets. OCR (Optical Character Recognition) OCR converts printed or scanned characters into machine-readable text. BCR (Barcode Reader) A barcode reader scans barcodes and converts them into digital information. MICR (Magnetic Ink Character Recognition) MICR reads characters printed with magnetic ink. It is commonly associated with processing bank cheques. Touch Screen A touch screen allows users to provide input by touching displayed objects or areas. Microphone A microphone converts sound into electrical or digital signals that a computer can process. Digital Camera A digital camera captures photographs and videos in digital form and can transfer them to a computer. 1.2.8 Output Devices Output devices present processed information to the user. Monitor A monitor displays text, images, video and other information. LCD Monitor LCD (Liquid Crystal Display) monitors use liquid crystals and a backlight to display images. They are thin and consume less power than older CRT displays. LED Monitor An LED monitor is generally an LCD display that uses LED backlighting. It is typically thin, energy-efficient and capable of producing bright images. Printer A printer produces a physical copy of digital information. Dot Matrix Printer A dot matrix printer is an impact printer that creates characters and images using pins striking an ink ribbon. Inkjet Printer An inkjet printer produces images and text by spraying tiny droplets of ink onto paper. Laser Printer A laser printer uses a laser and toner to produce high-quality prints quickly. Speaker A speaker converts electrical or digital audio signals into sound. 1.2.9 Hardware Interfaces Hardware interfaces provide connections between a computer and external or internal devices. Parallel Port A parallel port transfers multiple bits of data simultaneously. It was commonly used for older printers and other peripherals. Serial Port A serial port transfers data sequentially, one bit at a time. Traditional serial ports were commonly used for modems and other peripherals. USB Port USB (Universal Serial Bus) is a widely used interface for connecting computers to peripherals such as keyboards, mice, storage devices, cameras and smartphones. It can support both data transfer and power delivery. HDMI HDMI (High-Definition Multimedia Interface) is used to transmit digital audio and video between compatible devices, such as computers, monitors, televisions and projectors. Expansion Slots Expansion slots are connectors on a motherboard that allow additional hardware components, such as graphics cards, sound cards and network adapters, to be installed.
