Information Security and Cyber Law

8.1 Digital Society and Computer Ethics

A digital society is a society where computers, the Internet, and digital technologies are widely used in daily activities. Computer ethics refers to responsible and proper use of computers. Ethical issues include privacy, data protection, software piracy, plagiarism, unauthorized access, and misuse of information. Computer crimes include hacking, identity theft, fraud, and data theft. Security measures such as strong passwords, antivirus, firewalls, and regular updates help protect computer systems.

8.2 Information Security

Information security means protecting information from unauthorized access, use, modification, disclosure, or destruction. Common threats include viruses, hacking, phishing, malware, data theft, and unauthorized access. Encryption converts readable data into an unreadable form, while decryption converts it back into readable information using a key.

8.3 Cybercrime

Cybercrime is a crime committed using computers, networks, or the Internet. Common types include hacking, phishing, identity theft, online fraud, cyberbullying, data theft, and spreading malware. Cyber laws are laws designed to control and punish illegal activities involving computers and digital technologies.

8.4 Malicious Software and Spam

Malware is harmful software designed to damage systems or steal information. Common types include virus, worm, Trojan horse, spyware, ransomware, and adware. Phishing is an attempt to steal sensitive information through fake messages or websites. Spam refers to unwanted messages, usually sent in large numbers. Prevention includes using antivirus software, avoiding suspicious links, and keeping software updated.

8.5 Protection from Cybercrime

Cybersecurity protects computers, networks, and information from digital attacks. Important measures include using strong passwords, two-factor authentication, firewalls, antivirus software, software updates, secure networks, and regular data backups. A firewall controls network traffic, antivirus detects and removes malware, and backup provides a copy of important data if the original is lost or damaged.

8.6 Intellectual Property Rights

Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) protect creations of the human mind. Copyright protects original digital content such as software, books, music, and videos. Patents protect new inventions and technologies. Trademarks protect brand names, logos, and symbols. IPR encourages creativity and prevents unauthorized copying or use of digital content.

8.7 Digital Signature

A digital signature is an electronic method used to verify the identity of the sender and the authenticity of a digital document. It helps ensure authentication, integrity, and non-repudiation of electronic transactions.

8.8 Cyber Law in Nepal

Cyber law in Nepal provides legal rules for electronic transactions, digital signatures, computer-related offenses, and misuse of information technology. The major legal framework is the Electronic Transactions Act, 2063 (2008), which addresses electronic records, digital signatures, and various cyber offenses.

8.9 ICT Policy in Nepal

ICT Policy in Nepal aims to promote the development and use of information and communication technology in government, education, business, communication, and other sectors. It focuses on digital infrastructure, e-governance, digital literacy, cybersecurity, ICT education, and access to technology.
