Logic Function and Boolean Algebra

Introduction to Boolean Algebra

Boolean algebra is a mathematical system used in digital computers and electronic circuits to represent and simplify logical operations. It uses only two values: 0 (False) and 1 (True). The basic Boolean operators are AND, OR, and NOT.

Boolean Values, Truth Table and Boolean Expression

A Boolean value can be either 0 or 1. A Boolean expression is formed using Boolean variables and logical operators, such as A+B or A·B. A truth table shows all possible input combinations and their corresponding outputs. A Boolean function is an expression that produces an output based on one or more Boolean inputs.

Logic Gates

Logic gates are electronic circuits that perform logical operations. The AND gate gives 1 only when all inputs are 1 and its function is A·B. The OR gate gives 1 when at least one input is 1 and its function is A+B. The NOT gate gives the opposite value of its input and its function is A'. The NAND gate is the complement of AND, (A·B)', while the NOR gate is the complement of OR, (A+B)'. The XOR gate gives 1 when the inputs are different, while the XNOR gate gives 1 when the inputs are the same. Laws of Boolean Algebra

The important laws of Boolean algebra include Identity, Complement, Commutative, Associative, and Distributive laws. The Identity laws are A+0=A and A·1=A. The Complement laws are A+A'=1 and A·A'=0. The Commutative laws are A+B=B+A and A·B=B·A. The Associative laws are (A+B)+C=A+(B+C) and (A·B)·C=A·(B·C). The Distributive laws are A(B+C)=AB+AC and A+BC=(A+B)(A+C).

Verification of Boolean Laws

Boolean laws can be verified using a truth table. For example, to verify the Identity law A+0=A, we check both sides for all possible values of A. When A=0, both sides give 0, and when A=1, both sides give 1. Therefore, LHS = RHS, so the law is verified.
